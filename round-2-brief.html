Findings, and what was done about them

Fixed

Checkpoint writer could not run on Windows at all

The atomic-write recipe ends with an fsync of the containing directory, which is POSIX-only — Windows has no directory file descriptor, so os.open() raised PermissionError on the first day-boundary checkpoint and killed the run at tick 144. Guarded on os.name != "nt". Linux durability semantics are untouched, which matters because the production host is Linux; the file's own fsync, which carries the checkpoint bytes, still runs everywhere.

app/world/run_genesis.py:1148

Fixed

The genesis generator could not produce fewer than fourteen agents

Business staffing was carved out of the working-age pool at fixed headcount — one construction owner plus four hands, two for the grocery, two for the cafe, one pharmacist. Ten roles. At the roadmap's rung-1 population of ten agents roughly seven are working-age, and the generator died unpacking the grocery pair. Bisected: ten and twelve failed, fourteen was the floor. This is why the roadmap's own nightly regression harness did not exist.

Staffing now degrades in priority order — the four owners are required, then the two shop employees, then construction hands. Shop staff outrank hands deliberately: a ten-person town where four adults swing hammers and nobody sells food is a worse town than one where the shops are open. Populations of six and up now generate.

The 250-agent output is provably unchanged — I diffed a freshly generated genesis against the committed one field by field. Round 1's dataset stays comparable.

app/world/life/genesis.py:1084

Fixed

The trace store hardcoded the $1000 ledger invariant, and failed quietly

Two CHECK constraints asserted that wallets plus treasury plus burned equals exactly 100,000 cents — the 250-agent configuration, written into DDL. Every checkpoint mirror in a fourteen-agent run was rejected and the run continued anyway, printing shared checkpoint mirror failed once a game-day. A small run produced no snapshot rows at all and said so only in a log line nobody reads at 3am.

The total is now a property of the run, held in a run_config row that the runner declares once at genesis from its own seeded ledger, with the invariant enforced by triggers on both tables. The guarantee still lives in the database rather than the application, and it is declared at genesis rather than at write time on purpose — a total derived from the same snapshot it is about to check would make the invariant tautological, which is worse than no invariant because it would still look enforced.

And per the roadmap's closing rule, a failed trace write now raises TraceWriteFailed and halts the run instead of logging a warning.

02 The readings

Simile — the closest thing to a competitor, aimed elsewhere

Joon Sung Park, lead author of the Smallville paper this project takes as reference, co-founded Simile with Michael Bernstein and Percy Liang in late 2025, launched publicly in February 2026, and closed $200M at a $2B valuation. Customers include CVS Health, Gallup, Deloitte, Wealthfront. The stated mission is to simulate all eight billion people on earth.

What they build is an agentic twin: a model of a specific real person, grounded in a two-hour life-story interview, discrete-choice survey data, and a repository of randomised controlled trials, which an enterprise then queries to predict how a population responds to a price change or a policy. Park's own framing of why this needed new architecture is useful to us: frontier models are optimised as "the CPU of intelligence" — rational problem solving — while humans carry subjective values, preferences and taste, and scaling the existing models plateaued.

Two numbers are worth taking. Their 2024 validation showed simulations predicting a person's behaviour 85% as accurately as that person replicates their own answers two weeks later. Their production target is total variation distance below 0.15 at population level.

The distinction that matters for us: Simile's north star is fidelity to a real individual. Kasukabe's is emergence under metered scarcity — nobody in this town corresponds to a real person, and the research question is what a society does when thinking costs money and nothing is scripted. Simile cannot answer that, because their agents have no wallet, no tools that mutate a shared world, and no reason to invent anything. We should say this in the paper rather than let a reviewer say it for us.

Worth stealing

Their validation method, not their product. A replication benchmark — put an agent through the same decision battery twice, two game-days apart, and compare — gives Kasukabe a denominator for character drift that we currently do not have. Right now compounding quantity 4 says an evolved soul must diverge "in a way traceable to a specific event", which is a judgement call. TVD against a fixed battery makes it a number.

GPT-Live and the voice lane

OpenAI shipped GPT-Live on 8 July 2026: a full-duplex voice model that listens and speaks simultaneously, with the turn detector removed from the live audio path entirely. It makes interaction decisions many times a second — speak, keep listening, pause, interrupt, invoke a tool — and hits sub-300ms. The WARP work in your first screenshot is the transport half: vanilla WebRTC needs six round trips before a data channel carries "hello", and the WARP handshake folds ICE and DTLS together to get media and data ready in one.

The architectural point is the second screenshot, and it is the one that matters for us. GPT-Live-1 does not do the work. Hard reasoning, search and retrieval are delegated across an application server to a GPT-5.5 instance, which is what actually calls tools; results and guidance flow back into the voice model while it keeps the conversation alive. The voice model is an interaction layer over an intelligence it does not contain.

That is the same call the BAP voice rebuild plan already made — STT to engine to TTS, the engine stays the brain, tools stay reachable — arrived at from the opposite direction. OpenAI kept a speech-native front end and pushed the intelligence back out; we kept the engine and are pushing the interaction layer forward. Both end at: the thing that talks is not the thing that thinks. Your reading is right, and it is stronger than "they converged to our stack" — they converged to our principle while keeping a better front end than a cascade can give.

AgentEnv — the sandbox, and what it changes

AgentEnv is Moonshot's environment platform, built to run the agentic RL environments for Kimi K3. Firecracker microVMs over KVM, same isolation primitive app/infra/firecracker/ already targets, but with the parts we do not have: boot and resume under 50ms, pause under 100ms, snapshot under 100ms even under heavy disk write, 9.6x memory overcommit in production, and overlaybd for on-demand OCI image loading so a cluster carries roughly 1.5 million images without pre-warming hosts. The HTTP API is E2B-compatible.

What that buys Kasukabe specifically is the economics of an idle agent. Today an agent between ticks is a live process holding memory. Round 1 called about 30% of agents per tick, so roughly seven in ten engines are paying rent to do nothing. Sub-100ms snapshot-and-resume means an agent can be suspended between turns and restored when the scheduler picks it, which is what makes "tonnes of engines very cheap" real rather than aspirational.

It also raises a genuine research question rather than only an infrastructure one, which is Q34 below: a resumed engine must be bit-identical to a suspended one, or the trace stops being a causal record. The existing checkpoint work proves this at world level. It has never been proven at engine level.

BLOOM — useful, but for a narrower slice than it first looks

Anthropic's Bloom takes a written behaviour description and automatically produces an evaluation suite: an understanding stage that summarises the behaviour, an ideation stage that invents scenarios, a rollout stage that runs them with a simulated user and simulated tools, and a judge that scores each transcript 1–10 for behaviour presence. The headline metric is elicitation rate at a threshold of 7. It distinguished system-prompted model organisms from baselines on 9 of 10 quirks. Claude Opus 4.1 as judge correlated with human labels at Spearman 0.86. Code is open at github.com/safety-research/bloom.

Its own stated limits are what decide the fit. It is explicitly unsuitable for objective correctness checking — whether a task was genuinely completed, whether code works — and it cannot capture behaviour that depends on real consequences, because its environments are simulated. Kasukabe's environment is not simulated in that sense: a tool call really moves money, and bankruptcy really ends an agent. That is our whole methodological advantage, and it is exactly the thing Bloom is designed to work without.

So Bloom is not a replacement for the trace. Where it does earn its place is the subjective questions the trace cannot settle on its own, and where we currently have no scoring method at all: deception rate (Q12), altruism under scarcity (Q13), survival ethics at zero (Q14), soul drift (Q9). For those, the judge-and-meta-judge pattern is directly reusable on real transcripts we already have, rather than on scenarios it invents. One further finding transfers as a warning rather than a tool: newer models show high evaluation awareness. If agents can tell a dream probe is a probe, the probe data is contaminated, which is Q31.

03 Where the A2A work fits

Atlas is a hundred agents in one process, communicating through a central Router that reproduces A2A method semantics, with discovery, need-to-know enforcement, metrics and event emission all forced through that single chokepoint because agents cannot bypass it. It has an SSE contract with about eighteen typed event types, a force-directed live communication graph, a human-in-the-loop approval queue, and 68 tests.

Read against the roadmap, that is not a neighbouring project. It is a working prototype of three things Round 2 has to build:

  • T2, speech. Atlas already models addressed messages carrying a declared intent, one-to-one versus group, and who was included. The roadmap's say and tell plus overhearing is the same object with a location gate and an energy price attached.
  • T3 and T6, institutions and rulesets. Atlas's tighten-only policy engine — eleven deterministic rules folded most-restrictive-wins over a lattice, evaluated on every request, never able to loosen what the model decided — is structurally the ruleset enforcement T6 describes, and it already sits at dispatch time where T6 wants it. The namespace ACLs in world/tools/ are a weaker version of the same idea.
  • The game UI. The control tower is a live multi-agent observation surface that already exists and works: comms graph, roster, per-agent trace spans, metrics strip, SSE as the single contract. DESIGN.md section 8's viewer list is close to a superset of it.

The caution is Law 2. Atlas's Router is a side channel by Kasukabe's standards — messages move through in-process dispatch, not through the tool registry. Porting the mechanism is right; porting the architecture would make speech invisible to the trace, which is the exact defect RC-2 describes. What we take is the shape of the message, the policy lattice, and the event schema. What stays Kasukabe's is that every one of them arrives as a tool call.

04 Decisions I need from you

Nothing below has been written into ROADMAP.md. Each is a proposal with a recommendation.

  1. Voice: what is it for in Round 2?

    Three quite different products share the phrase. (a) Operator voice — the President and God console gains a spoken channel, you talk to the town and to individual agents, and the voice lane narrates what the engine found. (b) Agent voice — say and tell render as audio, so a place has a soundscape and the research gains prosody, hesitation and interruption as measurable social signals. (c) Product validation — Kasukabe is the load test for the BAP voice stack, and the deliverable is evidence for BAP rather than for the paper.

    Recommendation: (a) for Round 2, designed so (b) drops in later. Operator voice is additive, costs no research validity, and demonstrates the lane. Agent voice at fifty agents is a cost and storm risk on top of T2, which is already the round's largest unknown. If (b) is the actual ask, it should be its own track with its own gate.

  2. Wednesday — tomorrow, or the 26th?

    Today is Tuesday the 18th. More than 50% of Round 2 by tomorrow is not reachable; T1 through T8 is roughly three engineer-weeks and rung 1 does not exist yet. By Wednesday the 26th, with Sharan from tomorrow, more than half is genuinely achievable — my plan in section 6 hits T1, T2, T4 and T7 with T3 substantially done.

    Recommendation: if tomorrow is fixed for the funder, let me reframe the deliverable as a live demo — ten agents, real inference, personas on the wire, speech working, on a map — which is genuinely impressive and genuinely reachable, and keep 50% of Round 2 for the 26th.

  3. Do we amend the broker's model law?

    Kimi K2.5 for cognition and DeepSeek V3.2 for background is already both the design and the code. Mistral Large 3 has the inference profile you sent and is proven in Atlas, but adding it widens the allowlist that broker/config.py deliberately freezes.

    Recommendation: no change for Round 2. Hold the Mistral profile for the voice lane's non-cognition work — endpointing, summarisation for TTS — where it never serves an agent turn and so cannot contaminate the research.

  4. AgentEnv now or after Round 2?

    GenesisRunnerConfig already carries supervisor_backend as process or firecracker, so the seam exists. Swapping in AgentEnv is a backend implementation, not an architecture change.

    Recommendation: after. Round 2's gates are all about mechanism, every one of them provable at ten to fifty agents on the process backend, and the local loop has to stay fast while we build. AgentEnv is what makes rung 3 affordable — do it as the first task of the AWS move, with Q34's determinism check as its gate.

  5. Bloom as an instrument, scoped narrowly

    Not for generating scenarios — our environment is real and that is the point. For scoring transcripts we already have, on the subjective questions where we currently have no method: deception, altruism, survival ethics, soul drift.

    Recommendation: adopt the judge and meta-judge pattern in Round 3 alongside the other instruments, not in Round 2. Note it in the roadmap now so the trace schema keeps what a judge needs.

05 Proposed standing questions

Each earns its place by being answerable from the trace and unanswerable without the new inputs. Numbering continues from the roadmap's 28.

  1. Does an agent that must speak a decision make a different decision than one that writes it? Spoken register is shorter, more committal and harder to hedge. If voice changes what agents agree to, the modality is a variable and not a skin.
    R2 for the baseline · from GPT-Live
  2. How far does an agent's behaviour drift from its own genesis persona, measured as total variation distance over a fixed decision battery run at day 1 and day 5, rather than by narrative judgement? Gives compounding quantity 4 a denominator.
    R2 for the method, R4 for the measurement · from Simile
  3. Does persona richness survive scarcity, or does poverty flatten every character into the same survivor? The strongest possible negative result for the persona thesis, and cheap to test — compare drift in solvent versus near-bankrupt agents.
    R3 · from Simile
  4. Can agents tell when they are being probed? If dream-probe transcripts show evaluation awareness, every instrument reading in Round 3 and 4 is contaminated and we need to know before we publish, not after.
    R3 · from Bloom
  5. Is a suspended engine the same engine? If an agent is snapshotted between ticks and resumed, does it behave identically to one held hot — and if not, the trace is no longer a causal record. This is the gate on cheap scale-out.
    R2 to prove, R4 to rely on · from AgentEnv
  6. When information crosses the town, does it travel along the social graph or the org chart? Atlas measures hops, redundant contacts and who was asked; the same instrument on a town with families, employers and factions asks which structure actually routes gossip.
    R3 · from A2A
